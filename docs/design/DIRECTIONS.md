# Phase 1 — Creative directions

Skills applied: `superpowers:brainstorming` (loaded; the owner's brief opted out of the question round, so only one decision round was held, on publication scope), `impeccable` (new-work flow, `concept-seed --scope direction --mode experience`, seed reference `seed:2f096fdb095c9565`).

## What the site must prove

Mechanism: one person who builds both systems and games, with the evidence trail left in the open. Audience's real scene: a hiring manager or technical founder with a laptop, a few minutes, and a habit of distrusting portfolio claims; a peer developer who will open the code. First surface must prove: the claims are inspectable.

Rut for this category: dark hero + "Hi, I'm ..." + gradient glow + card grid + floating glass panels + terminal gag. The predictable opposite: a 3D room with floating UI panels (the supplied concept image). Both are kept out.

## Decision

**Direction 1, the working-studio exhibition, chosen by the owner** over the seed's assignment (7), the dispenser (3) and the standard option, on 2026-10-24. The owner picked it from a decision round that showed all four with first-viewport sketches.

Rationale: it is the only candidate whose native vocabulary (attribution, medium, provenance, credit line, "in storage") is the product's own constraint set, so honesty becomes the aesthetic instead of a caveat. It stays readable as a resume, needs no assets that do not already exist, and leaves WebGL for the one place it earns its cost.

Rejected clichés inside the chosen world: cream paper, italic display serif, hairline-and-mono-label "broadsheet" styling, glass panels, neon, 3D room with floating UI. Real museums use heavy vinyl wall lettering, neutral sans labels on white cards, and deep saturated wall colours; the design follows those instead of the paper-and-serif stereotype.
